# UK Employment Law Issue Map

Use this reference to identify the questions and evidence a UK employment matter may turn on. It deliberately avoids live rates, monetary limits, document lists, qualifying periods, and commencement dates; verify those through the current-law gate in `SKILL.md`.

## Jurisdiction and status

Start with territorial jurisdiction. Employment law in Northern Ireland differs from Great Britain. For Great Britain, distinguish employee, worker, genuinely self-employed contractor, agency worker, office-holder, and other special categories. Labels and contract wording are not conclusive: examine personal service, control, mutual obligations, substitution, integration, financial risk, and the reality of the arrangement. Different statutes may apply different tests.

Status affects entitlement, but do not assume that a non-employee has no rights. Workers may retain pay, holiday, working-time, discrimination, whistleblowing, and other protections.

## Recruitment and onboarding

Check non-discrimination throughout advertising, selection, offers, health questions, references, and pre-employment checks. Identify role-specific safeguarding, qualification, immigration, or health-and-safety requirements without turning a legitimate check into a blanket exclusion.

For right-to-work questions, identify the permitted check route, timing, evidence, repeat-check need, and record retention from current Home Office guidance. The employer owns the check and decision; software can structure evidence and reminders but should not declare a person's immigration status lawful.

Written terms may be spread across the principal statement, accessible policies, and later particulars. Verify who is entitled, what must be supplied, and when. Distinguish the statutory statement from the full employment contract and from non-contractual policies.

## Pay, deductions, time, and holiday

Pay questions may engage contractual pay, National Minimum Wage rules, equal pay, unlawful deductions, payslips, sick pay, family-related pay, tips, and payroll reporting. Identify worker category, pay-reference period, working time counted, deductions, accommodation or benefits, salaried-hours treatment, and records before comparing against a current rate.

Working-time questions may turn on average weekly time, night work, rest, breaks, young workers, mobile or sector-specific rules, on-call or travel time, opt-outs, and compensatory rest. Holiday questions require the worker pattern, leave year, statutory versus contractual allowance, accrual, carry-over, sickness or family leave interaction, irregular hours, termination, and current calculation rules.

## Leave and flexible working

Map the request to the correct statutory or contractual route: maternity, adoption, paternity, shared parental, parental, neonatal-care, parental bereavement, carer's, dependant emergency, sickness, jury service, public duties, or another category. Verify eligibility, notice, evidence, pay, protection from detriment or dismissal, return rights, and interaction with contractual enhancements.

Keep flexible-working requests distinct from general informal flexibility. Check the current statutory process, permissible refusal framework, consultation expectations, decision period, appeal or reconsideration, discrimination risk, and whether a reasonable-adjustment duty also applies.

## Equality, harassment, and adjustments

Identify the protected characteristic, alleged treatment, comparator or disadvantage, decision-maker, chronology, and workplace context. Consider direct and indirect discrimination, harassment, victimisation, equal pay, pregnancy and maternity protection, and disability discrimination separately; they have different tests.

For disability, examine knowledge, substantial disadvantage, reasonable adjustments, absence or capability procedures, occupational-health evidence, and alternatives. For harassment, examine prevention duties, third-party context, reporting routes, investigation, retaliation risk, training, and evidence. Do not treat a generic equality policy as proof that reasonable steps were taken.

## Performance, conduct, grievance, and absence

Classify the issue before choosing a process: misconduct, gross misconduct allegation, capability, ill health, attendance, performance, grievance, protected disclosure, interpersonal conflict, or redundancy. Mixed cases may need linked but distinct handling.

A defensible process usually needs a clear allegation or standard, impartial investigation, disclosed material, reasonable opportunity to respond, consideration of mitigation and alternatives, consistent treatment, a reasoned outcome, appeal, and reliable records. Check the Acas Code's applicability and current requirements. Avoid predetermined outcomes and suspension as a default punishment.

## Changing terms and business change

Identify the contractual term, variation mechanism, business reason, consultation and agreement history, affected group, collective or union arrangements, equality effects, and consequences of refusal. Continued work does not always establish unambiguous consent. Dismissal and re-engagement, deductions, mobility, hours, and pay changes carry distinct risks.

For TUPE, determine whether there may be a relevant business transfer or service-provision change, the affected employees, information and consultation duties, employee-liability information, measures, transfer-connected changes, and dismissal risk. Escalate early; TUPE is fact-sensitive and timing-dependent.

## Redundancy and dismissal

For redundancy, test whether the situation is genuinely redundancy, then examine the pool, selection criteria and scoring, alternatives, vacancies, consultation, collective thresholds, representatives, notification duties, notice, pay, appeal, and special priority rights. Do not use performance or conduct concerns as redundancy criteria without specialist review.

For dismissal, distinguish conduct, capability, redundancy, illegality, some other substantial reason, expiry of a fixed term, and automatically unfair or discriminatory reasons. Check contractual procedure, notice, accrued sums, written reasons, appeal, and consistency. Length of service affects some claims and remedies, not whether every dismissal risk exists.

## Ending employment and claims

On resignation, consider notice, waiver, garden leave, payments, property and access, confidentiality, restrictive covenants, references, holiday, final pay, P45/payroll handoff, and records. A resignation may still raise constructive-dismissal, discrimination, whistleblowing, or transfer issues.

For settlements or threatened claims, preserve documents and chronology, identify limitation risk immediately, avoid retaliation, and escalate. Settlement agreements require statutory formalities and independent advice for the worker; do not treat an informal waiver as equivalent.

## Product and control translation

An HR system can support lawful administration with structured facts, versioned terms and policies, reminders, workflows, access controls, immutable chronology, acknowledgement evidence, calculations with transparent assumptions, and exportable records. It should not silently decide employment status, immigration permission, disability, fairness, redundancy selection, reasonableness, or legal validity.

For every proposed feature, classify it as one of:

- factual record owned by the employer or worker;
- calculation based on explicit configurable inputs;
- reminder tied to a verified rule and effective date;
- procedural aid that preserves discretion and records reasons; or
- legal judgment that must remain with the employer or adviser.

Expose source date and rule version where the product ships defaults. Make thresholds and templates updateable, preserve historical values, and avoid presenting editable employer settings as the law.
