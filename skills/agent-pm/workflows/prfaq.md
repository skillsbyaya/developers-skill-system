# PRFAQ

Use a Working Backwards PRFAQ when a product idea needs a demanding customer-value and viability test before detailed requirements or investment. It is optional: do not require one when existing evidence and decisions already support the requested next step.

## Choose the operation

- **Create or update:** produce one coherent press release plus customer and internal FAQs from the current evidence and accepted decisions.
- **Validate:** challenge an existing PRFAQ and return a verdict and findings unless revision is requested.
- **One-pass or headless:** draft from supplied material without an interview, labelling assumptions and unresolved evidence. Require a specific customer, concrete problem and stakes, and solution concept; when one is materially absent, return `blocked` with the missing input instead of fabricating it.

When drafting, read [the PRFAQ template](../templates/prfaq.md). Keep the customer-facing claim plain and concrete; use the internal FAQ for difficult commercial, operational, technical, legal, and delivery questions.

## Evidence and research controls

1. Establish the target customer, painful problem, current alternatives, proposed experience, claimed benefit, source material, and decision the PRFAQ must support.
2. Separate verified facts, user-supplied evidence, estimates, assumptions, and aspirations. Every externally checkable market, competitor, behavioural, price, regulatory, or performance claim needs a current source or an explicit evidence gap.
3. Research only the questions that could change the promise, differentiation, feasibility, or verdict. Prefer primary and authoritative sources; record links near the claims they support. If current evidence cannot be obtained, weaken the claim or mark it unresolved.
4. Research supplies evidence, not the product verdict. This workflow integrates the evidence, resolves contradictions, and owns the final recommendation.

## Build and challenge

- Write the press release from the customer's perspective: who it is for, the problem, the new experience, concrete benefit, how it works at product level, and a credible call to action. Avoid unsupported superlatives and future certainty.
- Use customer FAQs to test adoption, switching, trust, accessibility, privacy, price, support, limitations, and failure recovery.
- Use internal FAQs to test strategic fit, differentiation, demand evidence, business model, cost and operational burden, dependencies, technology risk, compliance, abuse or safety, rollout, measurement, and reasons not to proceed.
- Resolve contradictions between the promise and the FAQs. A hard question must change the claim, scope, evidence plan, or verdict when its answer warrants it.

Adapt the challenge to the product type. For an internal tool, open-source project, or community initiative, replace commercial questions with adoption effort, workflow change, maintenance, sustainability, governance, and stakeholder value. In an interactive run, present the hard questions plainly and work through the answers; offer concrete alternative framings when the user is stuck. Toughness should improve the concept, not become performative gatekeeping.

## Verdict

Return one of:

- **Proceed:** the customer promise is specific and credible enough for the named next decision.
- **Proceed with conditions:** the direction is plausible, but named evidence or constraints must be resolved before a specified commitment.
- **Rework:** a material problem exists, but a bounded change could make the concept credible.
- **Stop:** the current concept lacks a defensible customer benefit or violates a material constraint.

State the decisive evidence, weakest assumption, conditions, and next decision.
