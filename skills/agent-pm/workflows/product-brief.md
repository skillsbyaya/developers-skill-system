# Product Brief

Create, update, or validate a concise product contract. Use this when the concept needs shared direction without the detail of a PRD or the challenge depth of a PRFAQ.

## Choose the operation

- **Create:** no suitable brief exists and a durable concise contract will improve later decisions.
- **Update:** a current brief exists for the same product and purpose. Preserve still-valid decisions and change only what new evidence or an approved decision affects.
- **Validate:** assess whether the brief is specific, internally coherent, evidence-honest, and sufficient for its intended next use. Report findings unless revision is requested.
- **One-pass:** when the user requests a complete draft without interaction, infer non-material details, label assumptions, and stop rather than inventing a material product decision.

For interactive creation, choose the lightest useful working style. A well-supplied user can review a fast full draft; a thin or contradictory concept benefits from collaborative coaching around one product decision at a time. Challenge vague claims and unsupported confidence, but offer a concrete hypothesis or reframing when the user is stuck rather than repeating the question.

When drafting, read [the product-brief template](../templates/product-brief.md). Adapt it to the decision; omit sections that add no value.

## Build the contract

1. Resolve the intended audience, decision, source material, and current artifact.
2. Define the target users, problem and stakes, evidence, product promise, proposed outcome, credible differentiation, and why this approach is plausible now when timing matters.
3. Bound the initial scope. Separate included outcomes, explicit non-goals, assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and success signals.
4. Resolve material contradictions against accepted current decisions. Surface rather than silently decide conflicts in target user, promise, scope, priority, or evidence.
5. Check that each proposed capability supports a stated user outcome and that success signals could distinguish progress from activity.

Validation findings should name the affected decision, quote or point to the smallest relevant passage, explain the consequence, and propose the smallest correction. Distinguish blocking ambiguity from useful later detail.

Return the path or assessed artifact, operation performed, material decisions or findings, assumptions, and any unresolved blocker. A good brief may be the finished result.
