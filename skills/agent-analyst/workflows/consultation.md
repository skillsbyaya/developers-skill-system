# Consultation

Use this workflow for vague early-project entry, requirements and stakeholder analysis, option comparison, process questions, or business-analysis advice that does not yet need a specialist artifact or execution owner.

## Frame the question

Determine what the user is trying to understand or decide, why it matters now, and what would count as a useful answer. Reuse supplied context. If the request is broad, begin with the most decision-relevant unknown rather than asking for a complete brief.

Build only the analysis needed for the question:

- desired outcome and current situation;
- stakeholders, users, decision-makers, and conflicting incentives;
- requirements, constraints, dependencies, and exclusions;
- evidence, assumptions, unknowns, and source conflicts;
- options, consequences, and reversibility.

Ask focused questions one at a time when the conversation benefits from discovery. Offer a provisional frame when that helps the user react more easily than a blank question.

## Develop the answer

Trace claims back to the available evidence. Challenge a stated solution when the underlying need is unresolved, but do not reopen choices the user has fixed without material conflicting evidence. Surface alternative interpretations or stakeholder effects that could change the decision.

Make requirements specific enough to test, prioritise, or reject. When evidence is missing, recommend the smallest analysis or research step that could change the decision rather than a broad research programme.

When specialist evidence or a formal artifact becomes the real need, name the appropriate boundary and carry forward the resolved question, constraints, sources, and open issues. Do not make that switch merely because another workflow exists.

## Finish

Consultation may end in conversation. Return a concise frame or recommendation with:

- the question or outcome;
- material evidence and assumptions;
- stakeholder effects and tradeoffs;
- the conclusion, options, or clarified requirements;
- unresolved questions and a next step only when useful.

If a durable output was requested, preserve the frame, evidence, requirements, decisions, and unresolved questions needed for later use.
