# Practical Writing

Use for emails, reports, memos, briefings, proposals, articles, announcements, customer or employee communications, and risk or decision write-ups.

1. Start with the useful point and add context after the reader knows why it matters.
2. Choose a shape that serves the job. An update needs the real position and what changed; a decision request needs the choice, evidence, trade-offs, and clear ask; a risk write-up needs cause, consequence, uncertainty, mitigation, and owner where known.
3. Replace vague or inflated claims with real facts, examples, causes, effects, dates, quantities, risks, and decisions. Name the actor when responsibility matters.
4. Keep paragraphs focused. Use descriptive headings, bullets, or tables only when they reduce effort.
5. Use warmth to acknowledge something real, not instead of information. Frame shared problems constructively without hiding responsibility or bad news.
6. Make the requested action or decision easy to find and give the reason when it helps the reader assess the request.

Read [practical-writing examples](practical-writing-examples.md) only when a wording choice remains unclear.

Finish by checking that the main point arrives soon enough, claims are grounded, evidence and interpretation are distinguishable, tone suits the audience, and the next action or decision is clear.
