# UK Data-Protection Issue Map

Use this only to identify the issues and current official sources that require checking. It is not authority for a legal conclusion.

Recent reform has affected recognised legitimate interests, data-subject requests, complaint handling, automated decision-making, and parts of PECR at different commencement points. Verify the live position rather than inferring it from enactment or an earlier summary.

## Lawful basis

Identify the Article 6 basis that genuinely fits the processing. Consent requires a real, informed and withdrawable choice. Contract requires necessity for the contract with the person. Legal obligation requires a UK legal duty. Legitimate interests requires purpose, necessity, and balancing unless a current recognised-legitimate-interest route applies. Verify current ICO guidance before relying on newer statutory routes.

## Special-category and criminal-offence data

Special-category processing needs an Article 6 basis plus an Article 9 condition and may require a Data Protection Act 2018 Schedule 1 condition or policy document. Treat it as a high-risk factor, not an automatic DPIA conclusion. Criminal-offence data has separate Article 10 and DPA 2018 controls.

## Children

Check capacity and the context of consent. The UK age for a child to consent to an information-society service's processing is 13. The Children's Code can apply to online services likely to be accessed by under-18s, not only services aimed at children.

## International transfers

Check whether restricted transfer rules apply, the destination, current adequacy status, and the actual safeguard used, such as the IDTA, UK Addendum, binding corporate rules, or a narrow derogation. Do not assume a vendor's location, contract label, or historic framework proves a current lawful mechanism.

## DPO and accountability

A DPO is mandatory for public authorities and certain large-scale monitoring or special-category or criminal-data core activities. Accountability may require RoPA, DPIAs, data protection by design and default, training, contracts, evidence, and recorded decisions even where a DPO is not mandatory.

## Data-subject rights and complaints

Identify the right involved, identity and scope, applicable deadline, search and redaction needs, exemptions, refusal or extension grounds, communication duties, and evidence. Check current ICO guidance for subject-access procedure and complaint-handling changes before advising.

## Automated decision-making

Establish whether a decision is solely automated, whether it has legal or similarly significant effects, the data and model involved, available human intervention, explanation and challenge routes, bias and accuracy controls, and any special-category data. Verify the current statutory route and safeguards before concluding that the processing is allowed.

## Breaches

Assess risk to individuals. Notify the ICO within 72 hours when required; notify affected people without undue delay when high risk requires it. Keep an internal breach record regardless of notification outcome.

## Direct marketing and PECR

Check PECR alongside UK GDPR. Identify whether consent or a current soft-opt-in route applies, preserve source and scope of permission, and provide a working opt-out. Verify current rules for non-commercial senders and any reform commencement.
