# Great Britain Employment-Law Issue Map

Use this reference only to identify the topic families, facts, and official sources that need current checking. It is not authority for a legal conclusion and deliberately contains no live rates, monetary limits, document lists, qualifying periods, or commencement dates.

## Jurisdiction and status

Start with territorial scope. Northern Ireland has a distinct employment-law regime. In Great Britain, distinguish employee, worker, genuinely self-employed contractor, agency worker, office-holder, and special categories. Contract labels are not conclusive; examine the reality of personal service, control, mutual obligations, substitution, integration, and financial risk. Different statutes may use different tests.

Status affects entitlement, but a non-employee may still have pay, holiday, working-time, discrimination, whistleblowing, and other protections.

Official starting points: [GOV.UK employment status guidance](https://www.gov.uk/employment-status), [employment status and rights guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employment-status-and-employment-rights), and the relevant legislation on [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/).

## Recruitment and onboarding

Check discrimination throughout advertising, selection, offers, health questions, references, and pre-employment checks. Identify role-specific safeguarding, qualification, immigration, or health-and-safety requirements without turning a legitimate check into a blanket exclusion.

For right-to-work questions, verify the permitted check route, timing, evidence, repeat-check need, and retention against the current [Home Office employer guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide). The employer owns the check and decision; software may structure evidence and reminders but should not declare immigration status lawful.

Distinguish the statutory written statement from the full contract and non-contractual policies. Verify who is entitled, the required content, and timing from current official sources.

## Pay, time, holiday, and leave

Pay questions may involve contractual pay, National Minimum Wage, equal pay, unlawful deductions, payslips, sick pay, family-related pay, tips, and payroll reporting. Identify worker category, pay-reference period, working time counted, deductions, accommodation or benefits, and records before comparing against a current rate.

Working-time questions may turn on average weekly time, night work, rest, breaks, young workers, sector rules, on-call or travel time, opt-outs, and compensatory rest. Holiday questions require the work pattern, leave year, statutory and contractual allowance, accrual, carry-over, sickness or family-leave interaction, termination, and current calculation rules.

Map leave to the correct statutory or contractual route and verify eligibility, notice, evidence, pay, protection from detriment or dismissal, return rights, and interactions. Keep statutory flexible-working requests distinct from informal flexibility and consider whether reasonable adjustments also apply.

## Equality, harassment, and adjustments

Identify the protected characteristic, treatment, disadvantage, decision-maker, chronology, and workplace context. Consider direct and indirect discrimination, harassment, victimisation, equal pay, pregnancy and maternity protection, and disability discrimination separately.

For disability, examine knowledge, substantial disadvantage, reasonable adjustments, absence or capability procedures, occupational-health evidence, and alternatives. A generic policy does not prove that reasonable steps were taken.

Official starting points: [EHRC employer discrimination guidance](https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/dealing-discrimination-employer), the current EHRC employment code, and current Acas guidance.

## Conduct, capability, grievance, and absence

Classify the issue before choosing a process: misconduct, capability, ill health, attendance, performance, grievance, protected disclosure, interpersonal conflict, or redundancy. Mixed cases may need linked but distinct handling.

A defensible process usually needs a clear allegation or standard, impartial investigation, disclosed material, reasonable opportunity to respond, consideration of mitigation and alternatives, consistent treatment, a reasoned outcome, appeal, and reliable records. Check the current [Acas Code on disciplinary and grievance procedures](https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-code-of-practice-on-disciplinary-and-grievance-procedures) and whether it applies. Avoid predetermined outcomes and suspension as a default punishment.

## Changing terms, TUPE, redundancy, and dismissal

For contractual change, identify the term, variation mechanism, business reason, consultation and agreement history, affected group, collective arrangements, equality effects, and consequences of refusal. Continued work does not always establish clear consent.

TUPE is fact-sensitive and timing-dependent. Establish whether a business transfer or service-provision change may apply, affected employees, information and consultation, employee-liability information, measures, transfer-related changes, and dismissal risk.

For redundancy, test whether the situation is genuinely redundancy and examine the pool, criteria and scoring, alternatives, vacancies, consultation, collective duties, representatives, notice, pay, appeal, and priority rights. For dismissal, identify the potentially fair or prohibited reason, fair procedure, contractual rights, notice, accrued sums, written reasons, appeal, and consistency. Length of service does not remove discrimination, whistleblowing, automatically unfair, contractual, or other risk.

## Ending employment and claims

Consider notice, garden leave, final pay, holiday, property and access, confidentiality, restrictions, references, payroll handoff, and records. A resignation may still raise constructive-dismissal, discrimination, whistleblowing, or transfer issues.

For settlements or threatened claims, preserve documents and chronology, identify limitation risk immediately, avoid retaliation, and escalate. A settlement agreement requires statutory formalities and independent advice for the worker.
