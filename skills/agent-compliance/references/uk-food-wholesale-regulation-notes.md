# UK Food and Wholesale Issue Map

Use this reference only to identify the topic families and official sources that require current checking. It is not authority for a legal conclusion and does not supply universal thresholds, temperatures, retention periods, mandatory fields, or approval categories.

## Registration, approval, and operator role

Clarify the food business operator, each establishment and activity, and whether the business manufactures, processes, packs, stores, transports, brokers, imports, exports, wholesales, retails, or serves food. Test ordinary registration separately from activity or product-specific approval, especially for products of animal origin and supply to other establishments. Include home, shared, mobile, office, and third-party premises where relevant.

Official starting points: the [Food Standards Agency registration service](https://register.food.gov.uk/new/), Food Standards Scotland, the relevant local authority, and applicable legislation.

## Food-safety management and hygiene

Map hazards and controls to the real product and process: supplier approval and specifications, receipt, storage, contamination and allergen control, processing, cooling, packing, dispatch, transport, cleaning, pests, maintenance, personal hygiene, training, complaints, and corrective action. Check the current HACCP-based requirement and the official small-business system relevant to the nation and operation.

Software may evidence checks and exceptions, but it does not validate a HACCP plan or make an unreviewed workflow effective.

## Allergens and food information

Classify the product and sales channel before deciding what information is required: prepacked, prepacked for direct sale, or non-prepacked; consumer or business customer; in-person or distance sale. Trace allergen facts to approved recipes, ingredient specifications, supplier changes, substitutions, cross-contact assessment, and product version.

For business-to-business supply, distinguish information the next food business needs to meet its duties from a consumer retail-label format. Do not assume a delivery note, web catalogue, verbal assurance, or precautionary statement is sufficient.

Official starting points: current [FSA allergen guidance](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses), Food Standards Scotland, GOV.UK labelling guidance, and applicable food-information legislation.

## Traceability, withdrawal, and recall

Test whether records identify relevant suppliers and business customers, products, quantities, dates, and lots, batches, or equivalent production units. Check internal traceability depth against risk and recall practicality. Preserve links between product or recipe versions, production, orders, dispatches, returns, complaints, and communications.

For an incident, separate withdrawal from recall, identify affected product and distribution quickly, contain further supply, contact the competent authority where required, and preserve a decision and communication trail. Never narrow scope merely because the software lacks complete data.

Official starting points: current FSA or FSS traceability and incident guidance and the relevant local-authority or regulator contacts.

## Storage, transport, delivery, and shelf life

Identify whether temperature control applies, the validated limit or procedure for the product, monitoring points, tolerances, corrective action, vehicle or container hygiene, cross-contamination and allergen segregation, delivery acceptance, and evidence. Verify numeric claims from current official guidance and product or process evidence.

Treat use-by, best-before, durability, freezing, thawing, and shelf-life decisions as product-specific safety or quality judgements requiring validation.

## Product, label, quantity, price, and claims

Depending on product and channel, test the responsible operator, name, ingredients and allergen emphasis, quantitative declarations, net quantity, durability date, storage and use conditions, address, origin, nutrition, lot marking, and category-specific particulars. Verify the exact mandatory particulars and exemptions rather than treating this list as a template.

Check weights and measures, consumer pricing, and the evidence behind nutrition, health, “free-from”, vegan, vegetarian, artisan, local, organic, provenance, or sustainability claims. Certification and customer specifications may add obligations beyond general food law.

## Imports, exports, packaging, and specialist categories

Escalate early for imports or exports, Northern Ireland movements, products of animal origin, alcohol, supplements, novel or fortified foods, organic certification, meat, fish or dairy rules, health claims, food-contact materials, feed, or cross-border online sales. Use commodity- and route-specific official sources rather than extrapolating from a domestic example.
