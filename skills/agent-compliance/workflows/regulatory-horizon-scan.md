# Regulatory Horizon Scan

Find, classify, and maintain material legal, regulatory, regulator-guidance, and standards changes that could affect the user's products, people, operations, or commitments.

## Establish the watch question

Use available context to identify:

- jurisdictions, nations, regulators, sectors, products, services, business model, workforce, data, safety exposure, and customer commitments;
- the decision horizon and relevant dates;
- current known obligations and accepted interpretations;
- existing watchlists, applicability findings, register entries, and specialist advice; and
- what consequence would make a change material.

Ask only for missing scope that would materially change where or what to monitor. A one-off “what changed?” request may finish in conversation; maintain a durable watchlist when monitoring will recur, deadlines must be tracked, or the user requests one.

When a current applicability finding exists for the same business and scope, use its applicable areas as the default scan boundary. Do not re-derive the whole compliance perimeter unless that finding is stale, contradicted, or materially affected by change.

## Source and status discipline

Search primary official sources first: legislation and official parliamentary material, government departments, regulators, enforcement or standards bodies, and official consultations or notices. Include material enforcement priorities, decisions, penalties, or thematic findings when they change credible exposure or expected practice. Use reliable secondary sources only to discover a lead or explain context, then verify the material claim at source.

For each item, distinguish:

- rumour or unsupported claim;
- policy statement or announced intention;
- consultation or call for evidence;
- draft legislation or proposed rule;
- enacted or adopted but not yet in force;
- commenced or currently in force;
- regulator guidance, code, or enforcement position;
- standards change or assurance expectation; and
- withdrawn, delayed, superseded, or no longer material.

Record publication, adoption, commencement, transition, response, and enforcement dates separately. Royal Assent, publication, or announcement does not prove commencement. State confidence and the exact next official check when status is unresolved.

## Assess materiality

1. Summarise the change without overstating certainty.
2. Identify who and what may be affected.
3. Compare it with current project scope, promises, records, controls, and accepted decisions.
4. Classify likely impact as monitor only, interpretation needed, register update needed, implementation planning needed, urgent operational action, or not applicable with reason.
5. Give the decision deadline, earliest sensible action, owner, and next source check.

Do not turn horizon monitoring into substantive legal advice or silently rewrite the compliance register. When interpretation is needed, hand the source, status, affected scope, deadline, and open question to the matching legal or compliance workflow. When a supported obligation changes, hand the decided delta to the obligations-register workflow.

## Maintain the watchlist

For durable monitoring, resolve the `regulatory-horizon-scan` row through [the convention-resolution rules](../../organise-docs/references/convention-resolution.md). Update the existing watchlist rather than creating dated duplicates.

Each live item uses a stable identifier and records topic, jurisdiction and authority, source links and dates, last verification, current status, materiality, affected scope, deadlines, owner, next check, confidence, trigger for escalation, and downstream handoff. Preserve a concise outcome for closed items that explains enacted, withdrawn, superseded, not applicable, or transferred status; remove detail that no longer helps future decisions.

When the watchlist uses project identifiers, resolve [the identifier convention](../../organise-docs/references/convention-resolution.md) and project area registry. Preserve existing identities and use canonical area suffixes for routing. Do not invent a new identifier prefix or number allocator for horizon items; use an already governed project type or retain the current watchlist identity.

## Finished result

Lead with changes that require action or a decision. Then give scope and search date, confirmed developments, uncertain or proposed items, deadlines, likely impact, next checks, downstream interpretation or register handoffs, and source limitations. Do not claim legal effect from an unverified summary.
