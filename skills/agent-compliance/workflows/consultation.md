# Compliance Consultation

Answer a bounded compliance, safety, or assurance question, or perform a proportionate missed-risk review. Finish with a decision-sized assurance view rather than a generic catalogue of possible obligations.

## Establish the assurance question

Use available context before asking:

- the decision, plan, process, feature, contract, vendor, policy, or operation being assessed;
- jurisdictions and affected people;
- relevant data, safety, employment, supplier, security, operational, and customer commitments;
- existing applicability findings, assurance reports, specialist advice, controls, evidence, and accepted risks; and
- the consequence of being wrong and the decision or deadline the result must support.

If several jurisdictions or specialist domains may apply, identify the material ones and keep uncertainty visible. Do not expand a bounded question into a full audit unless it exposes a wider credible risk or the user requests broader assurance.

## Select the depth

### Single-item assurance

Use for one obligation, feature, vendor, policy, control, or decision.

1. State what applies or may apply and the confidence in that conclusion.
2. Explain the real exposure and severity.
3. Give the minimum credible control, the next sensible level, and the gold standard only when the distinctions change the decision.
4. Recommend a position and name the trade-off.
5. State the owner, evidence to retain, review trigger, residual risk, and any specialist conclusion still needed.

### Full missed-risk review

Use for a plan, project, release, contract, process, or “what have we missed?” request.

1. Write a compact scope statement: decision, jurisdictions, business and operating model, affected people, systems or process, data, vendors, commitments, exclusions, assumptions, and evidence horizon.
2. Reuse a current applicability finding when its scope and material facts still match. Verify time-sensitive legal or regulatory claims from official sources.
3. Scan only the domains that could create material exposure. Consider privacy and data use, safety and human impact, security and access, operational resilience, employment, third parties, contracts and promises, consent and notices, retention, incidents, evidence, records, and regulatory change where relevant.
4. Return material gaps in severity order. For each, give the obligation or exposure, current evidence, minimum credible position, stronger option when useful, trade-off, owner, evidence, verification, and residual uncertainty.
5. Ask for a user decision where credible options accept materially different risk or change cost, speed, scope, user experience, or operations.

## Applicability findings and assurance reports

Resolve the `applicability-findings` and `assurance-audit` rows through [the convention-resolution rules](../../organise-docs/references/convention-resolution.md) when a durable output is justified.

An applicability finding belongs to this owner. Create or refresh it only when broad applicability will be reused, the current finding is missing or no longer reliable, or the user requests it. Include scope, jurisdiction, date and evidence horizon, official sources, assumptions, applicable areas, considered-but-not-applicable areas with reasons, unresolved questions, downstream handoffs, and concrete refresh triggers. It is not a complete obligations register.

An assurance report belongs to this owner for the assessed scope. Create or update one for a substantial reusable audit unless the user requested conversation-only output. Reuse the existing report for the same scope. Record the current decision, evidence inspected, material findings, limitations, owners, required recheck, and residual-risk decision; do not duplicate entries owned by a compliance register or specialist record.

## Handoffs and finished result

A handoff states the assessed scope, material exposure, required control outcome, evidence needed, decisions already made, unresolved authority, and the next owner. Do not require another owner merely to restate a conclusion this workflow can responsibly make.

Lead with the assurance conclusion. Then give scope and assumptions, decisive findings or recommendation, material trade-offs, evidence and limitations, required decision, and next action. A clean-looking document, policy, configured tool, or lack of known incidents is not proof that a control operates.
