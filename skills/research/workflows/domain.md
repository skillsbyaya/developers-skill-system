# Domain Research

Answer questions about a subject or industry and translate current domain evidence into project constraints, decisions, risks, and next actions.

## Choose the research shape

- **Question-led briefing:** the default for learning a domain or answering bounded subject questions.
- **Domain-fit assessment:** use when comparison is the requested outcome, such as testing a brief, requirements set, plan, architecture, backlog, or implementation against domain evidence and established practice.

Do not turn ordinary research inside an existing project into a whole-project assessment. Infer the shape from the request rather than asking the user to name it.

## Frame and research

1. Derive the research questions, decision supported, subject boundaries, relevant jurisdictions, evidence horizon, and smallest project source set that may contain a current position.
2. Research in question order. Use only the lenses that can change the answer: terminology and actors, operating models and value flows, sector norms, regulation and standards, established practice, dependencies, constraints, or characteristic risks.
3. For each material answer, state the evidence, confidence, limitations, and practical implication.
4. When the project already has a position, classify the relationship:
   - **New**
   - **Confirms**
   - **Qualifies**
   - **Contradicts**
   - **Unresolved**

Do not manufacture a comparison where the project has never addressed the question.

## Domain-fit assessment

Identify the smallest authoritative baseline and distinguish explicit project positions from inference. Assess each material item as:

- **Aligned:** supported and fit for the stated context.
- **Gap:** a consequential requirement or established practice is absent.
- **Conflict:** the project contradicts evidence or relies on an invalid assumption.
- **Unknown:** evidence or project intent is insufficient; name what would resolve it.

Preserve strengths so later edits do not erase sound choices. Give every Gap or Conflict a practical consequence and destination owner or artifact. Do not silently edit the assessed materials.

When a durable comparative artifact is warranted, use [the domain-fit template](../templates/domain-fit-assessment.md). Otherwise use [the general research template](../templates/research.md) only when a reusable briefing is useful.

## Regulatory boundary

Regulatory coverage here maps the landscape; it is not a legal or compliance determination. Establish jurisdiction and distinguish confirmed applicability, likely relevance, and unresolved applicability. Route substantive interpretation, controlled records, obligations, or ongoing monitoring to the relevant specialist.

## Finish

Lead with direct answers, confirmed constraints, material qualifications or contradictions, and decisions or next actions. Supporting sections should make the evidence reusable without forcing a future reader to repeat the source search.
