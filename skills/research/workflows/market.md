# Market Research

Answer a product or commercial decision with current evidence about markets, demand, customers, alternatives, competitors, and positioning.

## Frame the decision

Establish only what materially shapes the work:

- the decision or question;
- product, market, customer, alternatives, and competitors in scope;
- geography and time horizon;
- intended use and required confidence; and
- known constraints and existing evidence.

Scale the output to the need: a focused answer, a decision assessment, a supplied-source synthesis, or a reusable landscape report. Do not create a report merely because research occurred.

## Select decision-relevant lenses

Use only lenses that could change the decision:

- market definition, scale, growth, maturity, and structural change;
- customer jobs, observed behaviour, buying process, switching, and unmet needs;
- segments whose differences change product or go-to-market choices;
- direct, indirect, substitute, and status-quo alternatives;
- competitor capabilities, pricing, positioning, distribution, traction, and weaknesses;
- barriers, dependencies, channel dynamics, threats, and credible opportunity spaces.

Do not force demographics, personas, journeys, sizing, or competitor matrices when the evidence is weak or the decision does not need them.

## Evidence and comparison

- Prefer official statistics, filings, regulator or government data, company documentation and pricing, first-party disclosures, original surveys, and research with a disclosed method.
- Treat vendor marketing, paywalled-report summaries, search snippets, and forecasts cautiously. Show source incentives, method, sample, geography, and recency when they affect confidence.
- Customer claims require evidence. Label inferred segments, personas, demand, and buying behaviour as hypotheses rather than researched users.
- Reconcile incompatible market definitions before comparing figures. Use ranges or scenarios instead of false precision.
- Compare fair alternatives, including the status quo when credible.

Primary interviews, observation, prototypes, and usability tests belong to `agent-ux-designer`. Market research may identify what primary evidence is missing, but it must not fabricate it.

## Decide and finish

Lead with the answer supported by the evidence. Include only earned material: key findings, customer or segment implications, alternatives and competition, sizing or scenarios when relevant, opportunities and constraints, recommendation and strongest alternative, confidence, and the next evidence that could change the decision.

When a reusable report is warranted, use [the general research template](../templates/research.md) as an optional structure.
